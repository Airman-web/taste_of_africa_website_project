Chicken Tagine

  • Prep: 30 min
  • Cook: 1 hr 30 min
  • Serves: 4

A tagine is both a dish and the clay pot it is cooked in. The tall cone-shaped lid catches the steam and drips it back onto the food, so meat becomes tender with very little water. The method comes from the Amazigh (Berber) peoples of Morocco and was later enriched by Arab and Andalusian spices.

This version, djaj mqualli, pairs chicken with saffron, ginger, salty preserved lemon and green olives. In Moroccan homes the pot goes straight to the table and everyone eats from their side of it, scooping with fresh bread.

Ingredients

Servings: 4
  • 1200 g chicken thighs and drumsticks
  • 2 onions, grated
  • 3 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp ground ginger
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1 pinch saffron threads, soaked in 2 tbsp warm water
  • 1 preserved lemon
  • 1 cup green olives
  • 1 cup water
  • A handful of fresh coriander and parsley

Method

Step 1: Marinate the chicken
  1. Mix the garlic, olive oil, ginger, cumin, turmeric, pepper, saffron water and half the herbs.
  2. Rub the mixture all over the chicken and leave for at least 30 minutes, or overnight in the fridge.
Step 2: Build the tagine
  1. Spread the grated onion over the base of the tagine (or a heavy pot with a lid).
  2. Arrange the chicken on top in one layer and pour the water around it.
  3. Place a clay tagine on a heat diffuser. Sudden high heat can crack it.
Step 3: Slow-braise
  1. Cover and cook on low heat for about 1 hour 15 minutes.
  2. Check every 20 minutes and add a splash of water if the sauce looks dry.
Step 4: Finish and serve
  1. Rinse the preserved lemon, discard the pulp and cut the peel into strips.
  2. Add the lemon peel and olives and cook uncovered for 15 minutes so the sauce thickens.
  3. Scatter over the remaining herbs and serve with Moroccan bread (khobz) or couscous.
