North Africa

Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya and Egypt: where the Sahara meets the Mediterranean, and spice markets have traded for over a thousand years.

Food culture

North African cooking blends Amazigh (Berber) traditions with Arab, Ottoman, Andalusian and Mediterranean influences. Couscous, steamed grains of semolina, is so central that in Morocco it is the traditional Friday family meal, and it is recognised by UNESCO as shared cultural heritage of the region.

Hospitality matters: guests are welcomed with sweet mint tea, poured from high above the glass to make it foam. Dishes balance sweet and savoury, mixing meat with dried fruit, honey and nuts, and lifting it with saffron, cumin and salty preserved lemon.

Staple foods

  • Wheat and semolina
  • Couscous
  • Flatbread (khobz)
  • Chickpeas
  • Lentils and fava beans
  • Olives and olive oil
  • Dates
  • Lamb
  • Almonds

Key spices

Ras el hanout (a blend of up to 30 spices), harissa chilli paste, cumin, cinnamon, paprika and saffron.

Cooking techniques

Slow braising in a tagine
Meat and vegetables are slow-braised under a cone-shaped clay lid that drips steam back onto the food.
Steaming couscous
Couscous is steamed two or three times in a couscoussier above a bubbling stew, so the grains take in its aroma.
Preserving
Lemons are packed in salt for weeks, and butter is aged into smen, to keep food through the year.
Pit roasting
For mechoui, a whole lamb is slow-roasted in a clay pit until it falls off the bone.
