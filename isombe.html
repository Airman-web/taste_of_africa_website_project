Isombe

  • Prep: 20 min
  • Cook: 1 hr 45 min
  • Serves: 4

Isombe is one of Rwanda's best-loved home dishes. Young cassava leaves are pounded in a wooden mortar, then simmered for a long time with onion, small eggplants and creamy peanut paste. Neighbouring Burundi and the DR Congo cook close cousins called sombe and pondu.

It is cheap, filling and full of vitamins, so it appears at family dinners and at celebrations alike. Isombe is usually eaten with rice, boiled green bananas (ibitoke), sweet potatoes or ugali.

Ingredients

Servings: 4
  • 500 g pounded cassava leaves (fresh or frozen)
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, chopped
  • 2 small African eggplants, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 1/2 cup smooth peanut paste (unsweetened)
  • 3 tbsp palm oil
  • 1 tsp salt, or to taste

Method

Step 1: Prepare the leaves
  1. If using fresh leaves, strip them from the stems and pound them in a mortar (or pulse in a blender) until finely chopped.
  2. Frozen ground cassava leaves from an African shop are already prepared. Just thaw them.
Step 2: Boil long and slow
  1. Put the leaves and water in a large pot and boil for at least 1 hour, stirring often.
  2. This step matters: raw cassava leaves are not safe to eat, and long boiling makes them safe and soft.
Step 3: Add the vegetables
  1. Add the onion, garlic, eggplant and green pepper.
  2. Simmer for 20 minutes until the eggplant is soft.
Step 4: Make it creamy
  1. Loosen the peanut paste with a ladle of the hot liquid, then stir it into the pot with the palm oil and salt.
  2. Simmer for 20 to 30 minutes, stirring so it does not stick, until thick and the oil shines on top.
  3. Serve hot with rice, boiled green bananas or sweet potatoes.
