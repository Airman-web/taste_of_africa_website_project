Injera

  • Ferment: 2 to 3 days
  • Cook: 30 min
  • Makes: 8 injera

Injera is the daily bread of Ethiopia and Eritrea. It is made from teff, a tiny grain first farmed in the Ethiopian highlands thousands of years ago, which is naturally gluten-free and rich in iron.

A large injera is laid on a shared tray and covered with stews such as spicy berbere lentils. Diners tear off pieces to scoop up the food, and placing a bite into a friend's mouth, called gursha, is a sign of love and respect.

Ingredients

Injera: 8
  • 2 cups teff flour (brown or ivory)
  • 3 cups lukewarm water
  • 1/4 tsp active dry yeast (helps a first-time starter)
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • A little oil for the pan

Method

Day 1: Mix the batter
  1. Whisk the teff flour, yeast and water in a large glass or plastic bowl until smooth, like thin pancake batter.
  2. Cover with a clean cloth and leave at warm room temperature.
Days 2 to 3: Let it ferment
  1. Bubbles will form and the batter will smell pleasantly sour. That sourness is the flavour of injera.
  2. A layer of yellowish water may rise to the top. Pour most of it off.
  3. Stir in the salt and enough water to bring it back to a thin, pourable batter.
Cooking: Pour, steam, never flip
  1. Heat a large non-stick pan (or a traditional clay mitad) over medium heat and wipe it with oil.
  2. Pour the batter in a spiral, starting at the outside edge and moving to the centre.
  3. When holes ("eyes") cover the surface, put a lid on for about 1 minute to steam the top.
  4. The injera is ready when the edges lift. Do not flip it. Slide it onto a cloth to cool and repeat.
Serving tips
  1. Cool injera fully before stacking, or they stick together.
  2. Serve with misir wot (spicy red lentils), gomen (collard greens) or doro wat (chicken stew).
