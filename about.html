A short history of African cuisine

  1. Ancient farming: African farmers domesticated sorghum, teff, pearl millet, African rice, yams, okra and coffee thousands of years ago.
  2. Trade routes: Camel caravans across the Sahara and ships on the Indian Ocean brought spices, wheat and new cooking ideas, and carried African goods outward.
  3. New crops: From the 1500s, maize, cassava, tomatoes, peanuts and chilli peppers arrived from the Americas and quickly became African staples.
  4. Today: Cities from Lagos to Nairobi mix tradition with new ideas, while chefs in the diaspora carry African flavours to every continent.

Africa's influence on the world

African food shaped kitchens far beyond the continent. Enslaved Africans carried rice-growing skills, okra and black-eyed peas to the Americas, giving rise to gumbo, hoppin' John and much of soul food. Coffee, first used in Ethiopia, is now drunk everywhere.

Today, jollof, harissa, berbere and suya spice appear on menus in London, New York and Tokyo, and grains like fonio and teff are sold as "superfoods". We want people to know where these flavours come from.

Visit a food landmark: Jemaa el-Fnaa

Every evening, the main square of Marrakech, Morocco fills with food stalls selling harira soup, grilled meats, snails and fresh orange juice. The square is part of UNESCO's list of Intangible Cultural Heritage.
