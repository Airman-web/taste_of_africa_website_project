West Africa

Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Mali, Sierra Leone and their neighbours: a region of bright red stews, smoky grills and very generous portions.

Food culture

West African meals are built around a starch and a soup. A ball of fufu, pounded yam or banku is served next to a rich soup or stew, and pieces are pinched off with the right hand to scoop it up. Food is cooked in big pots because it is meant to be shared with family, neighbours and whoever drops by.

Flavour comes from palm oil, hot scotch bonnet peppers, smoked fish and fermented seasonings such as dawadawa (locust beans). Street food is huge too: think suya skewers in Lagos, kelewele (spicy fried plantain) in Accra and grilled fish by the beach in Dakar.

Staple foods

  • Rice
  • Yam
  • Cassava (gari, fufu)
  • Plantain
  • Millet and fonio
  • Black-eyed peas
  • Groundnuts
  • Okra
  • Egusi (melon seeds)
  • Palm oil

Key spices

Suya spice (yaji), grains of selim, alligator pepper, ginger, dried chilli and crayfish powder.

Cooking techniques

Pounding
Boiled yam or cassava is pounded in a large wooden mortar until smooth and stretchy to make fufu and pounded yam.
One-pot stewing
Rice, beans or meat are cooked directly in a reduced tomato and pepper base so every grain soaks up the sauce, as in jollof rice.
Open-fire grilling
Thin strips of beef are coated in peanut-based suya spice and grilled over charcoal.
Fermenting
Maize is fermented for kenkey and ogi, and locust beans for dawadawa, adding a deep, savoury taste.

Dishes to try

Jollof Rice

The famous one-pot party rice. Read our full recipe.

Egusi Soup

Ground melon seeds cooked with leafy greens, palm oil and meat or fish. Served with pounded yam.

Thieboudienne

Senegal's national dish of fish, rice and vegetables in tomato sauce, the ancestor of jollof.

Suya

Spicy grilled beef skewers sold at night markets across northern Nigeria and beyond.
