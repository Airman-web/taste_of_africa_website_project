East Africa

Ethiopia, Eritrea, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda and Burundi: highland grains, fertile green hills and a spice coast that traded with the world.

Food culture

In the Ethiopian and Eritrean highlands, meals are eaten together from one large tray lined with injera. Many families follow Orthodox fasting days with no meat or dairy, which is why the region has so many delicious plant-based stews. Meals often end with a slow coffee ceremony, because coffee was first discovered here.

Further south, in Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda and Tanzania, everyday plates pair a starch like ugali or matoke with beans, greens and grilled meat (nyama choma). On the Swahili coast and in Zanzibar, centuries of Indian Ocean trade brought cloves, cardamom and coconut into dishes like pilau and biryani.

Staple foods

  • Teff
  • Maize (ugali)
  • Green bananas (matoke)
  • Cassava and its leaves
  • Sorghum
  • Beans and lentils
  • Sweet potatoes
  • Coconut
  • Rice (on the coast)

Key spices

Berbere (chilli, fenugreek, coriander and more), mitmita, niter kibbeh (spiced butter), and Zanzibar's cloves, cinnamon and cardamom.

Cooking techniques

Fermenting
Teff batter is left to ferment for days to give injera its sour taste and bubbly texture.
Slow simmering
Ethiopian wats start by cooking onions for a long time without oil, then simmering them with berbere and spiced butter.
Pounding and boiling greens
Cassava leaves are pounded and boiled for a long time to make isombe in Rwanda and sombe in Burundi.
Steaming in banana leaves
In Uganda, green bananas are wrapped in banana leaves and steamed, then mashed into soft matoke.
